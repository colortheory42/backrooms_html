## SECTION 1 — GENERAL OBSERVATIONS

The structure presents as a multi-story interior office or commercial facility. Fluorescent lighting is consistent and functional throughout all surveyed areas. Carpet is present on all walking surfaces. Walls are a uniform yellow, approximately 240/220/80 in hue, with visible structural pillars at regular intervals.

The building is large.

When asked to clarify, this inspector can only restate: the building is large. Attempts to measure total square footage were abandoned after three hours of uninterrupted walking in a cardinal direction produced no exterior wall, no change in material, and no indication of distance from origin. GPS was unhelpful. The building does not appear to be aware that it has an exterior.

**Preliminary classification: Anomalous Interior · Scale: Unresolved**

## SECTION 2 — FLOOR AND SLAB VIOLATIONS

### 2.1 — Unprotected Floor Openings

**Applicable Standard:** 29 CFR §1926.501(b)(4)(i) — *Each employee on a walking/working surface shall be protected from falling through holes more than 6 feet above lower levels by personal fall arrest systems, covers, or guardrail systems.*

**Observed Condition:**

Floor openings were documented throughout Level 0 and in every subsequently accessed level. These are not construction holes. They are not temporary. They appear to be load-bearing voids — the surrounding slab continues uninterrupted; the opening simply exists within it, flush with the carpet, with no lip, no cover, no warning marking, and no guardrail.

The fall distances associated with these openings were measured in floor-levels rather than feet. One opening, documented at grid reference [UNABLE TO DETERMINE], dropped a surveying plumb line through what appeared to be no fewer than four distinct floor levels before the line ran out. The plumb bob was not recovered.

**Violation Class:** Serious  
**Probability of Injury:** High  
**Recommended Action:** Cover openings. Install guardrails. Determine why the building generated these openings and whether it intends to generate more.  
**Status of Recommendation:** Unresolved. The building does not appear to have a contact number.

---

### 2.2 — Floor Slabs of Anomalous Thickness

**Applicable Standard:** IBC §1604.3 — Serviceability. Structural systems shall be designed to have adequate stiffness to limit deflections and lateral drift.

**Observed Condition:**

The concrete slabs separating floor levels are unusually thick — estimated at 24 world units, which this inspector will describe as "substantial." From below, the underside of each slab is visible as a distinct ceiling surface. From above, the top of each slab is the walking surface. The gap between ceiling and floor belongs to the slab itself.

This is structurally correct. This is how a real building's floor works. The reason this is being flagged is that seeing it — seeing the mass of the concrete between you and the floor above — makes the building feel like it was built for a purpose other than occupancy. The slabs are not decorative. They are load-bearing separations between levels of something. What they are separating, and why there are so many of them, is outside the scope of this report.

**Violation Class:** None applicable  
**Note:** Flagged for atmosphere.

## SECTION 3 — VERTICAL SHAFT VIOLATIONS

### 3.1 — Multi-Story Open Shafts, Unguarded

**Applicable Standard:** 29 CFR §1926.501(b)(4)(i); ANSI/ASSE A1264.1 — *Safety Requirements for Workplace Walking/Working Surfaces and Their Access; Workplace Floor, Wall and Roof Openings.*

**Observed Condition:**

At irregular intervals throughout the facility, this inspector encountered vertical shaft structures measuring approximately 3×3 grid cells in plan and spanning a documented minimum of 6 floor levels in height. These shafts are not stairwells. They are not elevator shafts. They contain no ladder, no mechanical equipment, and no signage.

The shafts are open on all four sides at every floor level. The walls surrounding them are the same yellow construction material as the rest of the facility. At the base of each shaft, carpet continues normally, as if the floor at the bottom of a six-story void is a reasonable place to stand.

Standing at the base of a shaft and looking up, this inspector observed the undersides of multiple concrete slabs receding into the distance. The lighting does not reach the top. This is because the top is very far away.

**Per §1926.501(b)(4)(i)**, employees working near openings more than 6 feet above lower levels must be protected by guardrail systems, safety net systems, or personal fall arrest systems. These shafts have none of these. They have walls, which in some cases have been partially removed by a previous occupant or by the building itself — it is not clear which.

**Violation Class:** Willful  
**Probability of Injury:** Extreme  
**Recommended Action:** Install guardrails at all shaft perimeter openings on all affected floor levels. Determine how the shafts were generated. Determine why the building generated structures that have no purpose except to be very tall indoor air.

---

### 3.2 — Wall Openings at Shaft Perimeter, Below 39 Inches

**Applicable Standard:** 29 CFR §1910.28 — *Each employee near an opening where the inside bottom edge is less than 39 inches above the walking surface and the outside bottom edge is 4 feet or more above a lower level must be protected from falling.*

**Observed Condition:**

Windows are present in the shaft perimeter walls at multiple floor levels. These windows begin approximately 45% of the way up the wall and terminate at 80%. They are not glazed. They are openings. From inside a shaft-adjacent room, one can look through these openings and observe the interior of the shaft — the void, the distant carpet below, the slab edges of other floors visible in cross-section.

From inside the shaft, if one were somehow there, one could look through the same openings into the rooms.

This inspector does not know how one would get into the shaft without falling. This is noted as a secondary concern given that the primary concern is that the shaft is there at all.

**Violation Class:** Serious  
**Recommended Action:** Glaze or cover all shaft perimeter window openings. Alternatively, remove the shafts. Alternatively, determine whether the shafts are load-bearing and whether removing them would affect the structural integrity of an infinitely large building, which is itself a question this report is not equipped to answer.

## SECTION 4 — SCALE AND CLASSIFICATION VIOLATIONS

### 4.1 — Building Scale Incompatible With Intended Occupancy

**Applicable Standard:** IBC §302 — Occupancy Classification. Buildings shall be classified by their primary occupancy type and designed accordingly.

**Observed Condition:**

This facility presents visual and material cues consistent with commercial office occupancy — carpet, drop ceilings, fluorescent lighting, painted walls. The scale is not consistent with commercial office occupancy.

The ceiling height observed in standard rooms ranges from approximately 220 to 700 world units depending on floor level. A standard commercial office ceiling height is 9 to 10 feet. The upper end of the observed range is approximately equivalent to a seven-story building inside a single room.

This inspector initially believed this to be a rendering error. It is not a rendering error. The building is simply this tall inside. The carpet does not change. The lights do not change. The walls do not change. Only the quantity of them changes, which is to say, there is significantly more interior than is normal.

**Violation Class:** Unclassifiable  
**Note:** There is no building code violation for being too big indoors. This inspector recommends one be written.

---

### 4.2 — Architecture Consistent With Procedural Generation Error

**Applicable Standard:** None applicable. This is a finding, not a violation.

**Observed Condition:**

Based on the totality of observations — infinite walkable area, consistent materials, irregular void placement, floor-level variability, shaft structures of no apparent purpose — this inspector has concluded that the building was not designed. It was generated. Something produced it according to a set of rules, and in some areas, the rules produced an error.

The shafts are where the count went wrong. The floor that should be on level 4 is on level 3 instead, or not at all, and the space between them is just air with walls around it because the walls were part of a different calculation. The building did not intend to create a six-story indoor void. The building did not intend anything. It computed, and the computation produced this, and then it kept going.

The building is not haunted. The building made a mistake and the mistake is structural and the mistake is six floors tall and you are standing at the bottom of it on carpet under fluorescent lights and the slab above you is 24 units thick and very real.

**Recommended Action:** None available.

## SECTION 5 — INSPECTOR'S NOTES

This is my fourteenth hour in the building. The lighting has not changed. The hum — a 60-cycle tone with harmonics at 120, 180, and 240 — has not changed. I have not found a stairwell, only a series of floor-level transitions that appear when you walk over them, and shaft interiors if you fall.

I have been in many buildings. Buildings have edges. Buildings have a moment where the material ends and the outside begins. I have not found that moment here. I am beginning to suspect the moment does not exist.

The violations documented in this report are real violations. The fall hazards are real fall hazards. A person could step into one of these floor openings and fall through four levels of a real concrete building and land on real carpet and it would be a real injury.

The fact that this building should not exist does not mean it is safe.
