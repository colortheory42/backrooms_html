# THE BACKROOMS

---

> *You weren't supposed to find this.*

---

Somewhere behind the walls of every office building, every fluorescent-lit corridor, every room you've ever walked through without thinking — there is another place.

It doesn't have doors. It doesn't have windows. It doesn't have an end.

The carpet is blue. The walls are yellow. The lights hum at a frequency that feels like it's meant to calm you, but doesn't. You've been walking for what might be hours. The geometry doesn't add up. There are more rooms ahead of you than there were behind you. There always are.

---

## Where You Are

**Level 0** is the entry point. Infinite procedurally generated rooms, each one identical to the last in the way that a nightmare is identical to the one before it — same materials, different shape, never quite where you left it. The ceiling is low. The pillars repeat. Some walls have doorways. Some walls have been removed by whoever was here before you.

Some floors have no bottom.

The deeper you go, the more the geometry begins to change. Floors that should be solid aren't. Rooms that should have ceilings open into vertical shafts that run through multiple levels, dark columns of nothing surrounded by walls you can punch through if you're desperate enough. Below those shafts, at the very bottom, the carpet continues as if nothing happened. Above, the void.

You can fall.

---

## What This Place Is

This is a mathematical object. It was not built room by room — it was derived. Every wall, every gap, every crumbling doorway is the output of a deterministic function seeded from a single 64-bit number. The same seed produces the same infinite world, every time, forever. You could walk for a hundred hours and never reach the edge, because there is no edge. You could return to the exact coordinates where you started and find it unchanged, because it was never stored — it was calculated.

The hum you hear is real. It's a 60Hz oscillator with harmonic overtones, amplitude-modulated at 0.08Hz, feeding into a synthetic reverb. It changes as you move — more open in wider rooms, tighter in corridors. The footsteps are carpet thuds: filtered noise, no highs, just the low pressure of something soft absorbing the impact of your weight.

The lights don't flicker. That's the only mercy.

---

## The Shafts

Some zones contain vertical voids — shafts carved through six floors of geometry, open from top to bottom. The walls around them are thick and yellow, punctuated with windows and doorways at irregular intervals. You can stand at the edge and look down into the levels below you, see the carpet at the bottom, count the floors.

You can also remove those walls.

The border walls of a shaft respond to force the same way regular walls do. One section at a time, each the width of a grid cell, can be punched out. What's behind them is air. What's below that air, eventually, is a floor you can land on — or isn't, and you fall until the game decides you've fallen far enough.

---

## The Rules

- **The floor is not always there.** Some cells have no slab. You will find this out by falling.
- **The walls are not always solid.** Open zones exist where the density drops and walls become suggestions. Some of them were removed by you in a previous session. Some were never there.
- **The geometry is consistent.** If you walk back to where you were, it will be the same. The destroyed walls will still be destroyed. The holes will still be holes.
- **There is a bottom to every shaft.** The carpet is always there, at the lowest floor. Whether you survive reaching it is a separate question.

---

## On Being Here

You are not supposed to understand this place. You are supposed to move through it. The rooms are not trying to tell you anything. The hum is not a message. The way the walls catch the light at certain angles, the way the perspective compresses at distance into amber fog — none of this means anything.

It just is.

It has always just been.
